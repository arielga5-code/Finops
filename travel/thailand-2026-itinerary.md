# Thailand — 21 August to 19 September 2026

**Traveller:** solo · **Passport:** Israeli · **Budget:** mid-range (฿1,500–3,000/night)
**Duration:** 29 nights / 30 days · **Route:** Bangkok → Pattaya → Chiang Mai → Pai → Chiang Rai → Bangkok

---

## The one decision that shapes everything: weather sequencing

You are travelling in the back half of the monsoon. The rain is not a reason to cancel — it is
mostly short, violent afternoon downpours, not all-day drizzle — but the *order* of your stops
matters a lot:

| | Late August | September |
|---|---|---|
| **Pattaya** | ~98 mm rain, 12 rain days, sea ~29 °C | ~205 mm — the wettest month of the year |
| **Chiang Mai** | Very wet | ~249 mm, ~18 rain days — also peak |

Pattaya is the weather-sensitive stop (beach, boats, islands — all ruined by rain).
Chiang Mai is weather-*tolerant* (temples, cooking classes, cafés, massage courses, markets —
and waterfalls are actually *better* in the wet).

**So: beach first while it's driest, mountains second.** That is why this itinerary puts Pattaya
in late August and the north in September, not the other way around.

The upside nobody mentions: this is low season. You will get 4-star rooms at 3-star prices,
empty temples, no queues at the Grand Palace, and a Chiang Mai that is *green* — the polar
opposite of the smoke-choked burning season (Feb–Apr).

## Phase 1 — Bangkok, 3 nights (21–24 Aug)

**Stay:** Riverside/Old Town (Phra Nakhon) for atmosphere, or Sukhumvit around Asok/Phrom Phong
(Sukhumvit BTS line, Asok–Thong Lo) for convenience and food. Solo: Sukhumvit — you'll use the
BTS constantly and it's the easiest to be alone in.

- **Fri 21 — arrival.** Do nothing ambitious. Airport Rail Link or a Grab into town, shower,
  a bowl of noodles, an early night. Fighting jet lag on day one wrecks days two and three.
- **Sat 22 — Chatuchak Weekend Market** (morning, before the heat; 15,000 stalls, weekends only —
  this is why your Friday arrival is lucky). Afternoon: Jim Thompson House or a spa. Evening:
  Yaowarat (Chinatown) street food after dark.
- **Sun 23 — the temple run.** Grand Palace + Wat Pho (go at 08:30 opening, long trousers and
  covered shoulders, absolutely mandatory), cross the river by ฿5 ferry to Wat Arun. Sunset
  from a rooftop bar or a ฿15 Chao Phraya express boat ride.
- **Mon 24 —** morning at Talad Noi or Wat Saket (Golden Mount) if energy allows, then travel
  to Pattaya.

**Getting to Pattaya:** Bell Travel or Roong Reuang bus from Ekkamai Eastern Bus Terminal
(~฿130–250, 2–2.5 h), or a Grab/private car (~฿1,500–2,000, 2 h door to door). If you'd rather
skip Bangkok's centre entirely on arrival day, there is a direct bus from Suvarnabhumi Airport
to Pattaya — but you'd lose the Chatuchak weekend, so I'd keep the Bangkok stop.

## Phase 2 — Pattaya, 8 nights (24 Aug – 1 Sep)

Pattaya has a reputation. Ignore it — it's entirely avoidable. The city has three completely
different faces and you get to pick which one you stay in.

**Where to stay (solo, mid-range):**
- **Pratamnak Hill** — my pick. The quiet headland *between* Central Pattaya and Jomtien.
  Good hotels, sea views, Cosy Beach, 10 minutes from either side. Calm without being isolated.
- **Jomtien** — long flat beach 5 km south, relaxed, lots of cafés, far more residential.
- **Central Pattaya** — convenient and lively, walkable to everything, but you're near the noise.
- Avoid booking directly on/behind Walking Street unless that's specifically what you want.

**Things worth your time:**
- **Sanctuary of Truth** — an enormous all-teak carved temple on the sea, still under
  construction after 40 years. Genuinely extraordinary. Go early morning.
- **Koh Larn (Coral Island)** — ferry from Bali Hai pier (~฿30, 45 min) or speedboat. Samae and
  Tien beaches are the good ones. **Check the sea forecast the night before** — this is the
  activity most likely to get rained out, so keep it flexible and grab the first clear day.
- **Nong Nooch Tropical Garden** — 500 acres of landscaped gardens; far better than it sounds.
- **Wat Phra Yai (Big Buddha)** and the **Pratamnak viewpoint** for sunset.
- **Thepprasit Night Market** (Fri–Sun) — the good local one, not the tourist one.
- **Silverlake Vineyard**, **Khao Chi Chan (Buddha Mountain)**, **Khao Kheow Open Zoo** — an
  easy half-day trio inland if you rent a car or take a Grab.
- **Rain day fallbacks:** Terminal 21 / Central Festival malls, Art in Paradise, Ramayana
  Water Park (fun in the rain anyway), a 2-hour Thai massage for ฿400.

**Optional 2-night add-on:** if you want a proper island, **Koh Samet** is ~2 h from Pattaya
(bus to Ban Phe + ferry) and sits on the Gulf coast, which holds up far better in the monsoon
than the Andaman side. Slot it 27–29 August. Say the word and I'll rework the dates.

## Phase 3 — Chiang Mai, 12 nights across three blocks (1–18 Sep)

**Getting there:** first check **U-Tapao (UTP) → Chiang Mai (CNX)** direct — U-Tapao is only
40 minutes from Pattaya and Thai AirAsia/Thai Vietjet run this route seasonally. If it isn't
flying on 1 September, fall back to a bus to Suvarnabhumi (~2 h) and fly BKK→CNX, or Don Mueang
(DMK→CNX) for the cheapest fares. Budget ฿1,200–2,500 one-way booked now.

**Where to stay:**
- **Old City (inside the moat)** — temples on every corner, walkable, best for a first visit.
  Nimmanhaemin end for cafés, Tha Phae Gate end for markets.
- **Nimmanhaemin ("Nimman")** — coffee, co-working, modern restaurants, younger crowd. Best
  base if you like being in a neighbourhood rather than a sight.
- **Riverside (Charoenrat Rd)** — quieter, prettier, needs a scooter or Grab.

At your budget you can get a very good condo with pool and gym on a monthly rate. Ask hotels
directly for a "long stay rate" — booking sites don't show it.

### Block A, 1–8 Sep (7 nights)
- **Doi Suthep temple** at sunrise (before the tour buses; ~40 min up the mountain).
- **Old City temple walk** — Wat Chedi Luang, Wat Phra Singh, Wat Chiang Man. A morning on foot.
- **A Thai cooking class** (฿1,000–1,500, full day with market tour). The single best-value
  thing you'll do in Thailand. Book one that includes the market.
- **Elephant sanctuary** — go *only* to a genuine no-riding, no-bathing-circus sanctuary
  (Elephant Nature Park is the benchmark). Book 2–3 weeks ahead, they sell out even in low season.
- **Bua Thong "Sticky" Waterfalls** — limestone you can literally walk up, free, and
  spectacular in the wet season. Rainy season is the *right* time for this.
- **Sun 6 Sep — Sunday Walking Street** on Ratchadamnoen Rd, the big one. (Saturday's Wua Lai
  street market runs 5 and 12 Sep.)
- **A Muay Thai fight** at Thapae Boxing Stadium, or a 5-day Thai massage course at TMC if you
  want a skill to take home.

### Block B — Pai, 8–11 Sep (3 nights)
Minivan from Arcade Bus Station, ~฿200, 3–4 h, **762 curves**. Take motion-sickness tablets
before you board — this is not a joke, people vomit on this road every single day. Sit in the
front row.

Pai in September is at its most beautiful and its most washed-out: rice terraces glowing green,
waterfalls at full force, and roads that can genuinely land-slip. Pai Canyon, Pam Bok waterfall,
the bamboo bridge (Boon Ko Ku So), Tham Lod cave, hot springs, and a town that runs on
banana pancakes and live music.

**Do not rent a scooter here unless you are an experienced rider.** The Pai road and the wet
season together are how tourists end up in Chiang Mai hospital with "Pai tattoos". Use songthaews.

### Block C — Chiang Rai, 13–15 Sep (2 nights)
Green Bus from Chiang Mai Arcade (~฿170–290, 3–3.5 h, book the V-Class). **Wat Rong Khun**
(the White Temple), **Wat Rong Suea Ten** (the Blue Temple), **Baan Dam** (the Black House),
Choui Fong tea plantation, and the Golden Triangle on the Mekong if you want the extra day trip.

### Block D — Chiang Mai, 15–18 Sep (3 nights)
Deliberately unplanned. By now you'll know what you want more of. Doi Inthanon (Thailand's
highest peak) is the obvious remaining big day trip. Otherwise: cafés in Nimman, Baan Kang Wat
artists' village, a last massage, and shopping for the flight.

## Phase 4 — the way home (18–19 Sep)

Fly **CNX → BKK on Friday 18 September**, sleep near Suvarnabhumi, fly out Saturday 19th.

**Do not** book a same-day domestic connection onto a separate international ticket. One
thunderstorm in Chiang Mai and you miss an 11-hour flight home with zero protection, because
separate tickets means the airline owes you nothing. The buffer night costs ฿1,500. Skipping it
can cost $800.

## Entry requirements — Israeli passport

- **Visa:** Israel currently holds a **60-day visa exemption** on arrival. Your trip is 30 days,
  so you are comfortably inside it. ⚠️ A Thai cabinet decision of 19 May 2026 approved cutting
  the exemption to **30 days**; as of late June 2026 it had not been published in the Royal
  Gazette and was therefore not yet law. Either way your trip fits — but **read your entry stamp
  at the airport** and confirm the "admitted until" date rather than assuming. Re-check
  mfa.go.th about a week before you fly.
- **Passport validity:** 6 months beyond entry.
- **TDAC (Thailand Digital Arrival Card):** mandatory for every entry since 1 May 2025, replaces
  the old paper TM6. Free, online at **tdac.immigration.go.th**, and you can only submit it
  **within 72 hours of arrival** — so around 18–19 August, not before. Save the QR code offline;
  you may be asked for it during your stay, not just at the border.
- **Proof of onward travel:** you need a ticket out of Thailand. Airlines check this at
  check-in in Tel Aviv more reliably than Thai immigration does. Have your 19 September
  booking on your phone.
- **Beware the "free e-visa" sites.** TDAC is free and the only official site is
  tdac.immigration.go.th. Everything charging you €40 for it is a middleman.

## Health

- **Dengue is the real risk**, not exotic diseases. Peak season is May–September, and the
  *Aedes* mosquito that carries it **bites during the day**, especially dawn and dusk — so
  repellent is a daytime habit, not an evening one. Use **DEET 30–50%** or picaridin.
- **If you get a sudden high fever with pain behind the eyes: take paracetamol only.**
  Never ibuprofen, aspirin or naproxen — they thin the blood and turn dengue dangerous.
  Get to a hospital; Bangkok Hospital Pattaya and Chiang Mai Ram are both excellent.
- **Vaccines:** make sure routine ones are current (tetanus, MMR, hepatitis A). Hepatitis B and
  typhoid are commonly recommended for a month-long trip. Rabies pre-exposure is worth
  discussing with a travel clinic if you'll be near animals — and either way, **never touch the
  street dogs or the monkeys.** A monkey scratch means a hospital visit, not a plaster.
- Book a travel-clinic appointment **this week** — some vaccines need a lead time and you're
  3.5 weeks out.
- **Insurance — read the motorbike clause.** Most Israeli policies (Harel, Passportcard, Clal)
  void medical cover for scooter accidents unless you hold a valid motorcycle licence *and*
  wear a helmet. Scooter crashes are by far the most common serious injury for tourists in
  Thailand. If you intend to ride at all, buy a policy that explicitly covers it.
- **Stomach:** don't drink the tap water (ice in restaurants is factory-made and fine). Busy
  street stalls with high turnover are safer than empty restaurants.

## Laws that catch Israelis out

- **🚭 Vapes are completely illegal.** Not "frowned upon" — illegal to possess, use, or import,
  nicotine-free included. Fines to ฿30,000, and importing one falls under the Customs Act with
  a theoretical 10-year maximum. Thai police opened a dedicated E-Cigarette Control Centre on
  29 June 2026 and ran a nationwide crackdown through mid-July. **Leave it at home. Do not
  put it in your checked bag.** This is the single most common way tourists get shaken down.
- **Cannabis** rolled back toward medical-only in late 2025. Dispensaries are open but now
  require a consultation form or ID; **public smoking carries fines up to ฿25,000**. Buy only
  from licensed shops, never from a street seller, and never consume in public.
- **E-cigarettes aside:** never disrespect the King or the royal family — lèse-majesté is
  prosecuted seriously, including for social media posts.
- Don't buy or carry Buddha images out of the country without permission; it's technically
  an export offence.

## Scams and safety

Thailand is very safe. Violence against tourists is rare; the risk is your wallet and your
scooter. Knowing these five covers almost everything:

1. **Jet ski scam (Pattaya's signature).** You return the ski, they point at old damage and
   demand ฿30,000. Solution: **don't rent jet skis.** If you must, film a slow full walk-around
   of the machine with the operator visible before you get on.
2. **"The Grand Palace is closed today."** It isn't. A friendly stranger redirects you to a
   tuk-tuk to a gem shop / tailor. Walk on.
3. **Tuk-tuk and taxi fares.** In Bangkok insist on the meter or just use **Grab** (or Bolt) —
   it's cheap, GPS-tracked and removes the negotiation entirely. In Pattaya, shared songthaews
   are ฿10–20 a hop; the moment you say "take me to X" it becomes a private charter and the
   price is ฿200+. Agree the fare before you get in.
4. **Motorbike rental passport hostage.** Never leave your passport as a deposit — leave cash
   or a copy. Recovering a passport from a dispute is a nightmare. Also: **an International
   Driving Permit with the motorcycle category is legally required**, and without it your
   insurance is void and the police checkpoint fine (฿500–1,000) is routine.
5. **Bar bill padding / ping-pong show touts.** If someone on the street is enthusiastically
   offering you a show, it ends in a ฿5,000 bill and a bouncer. Just don't.

**Solo-specific:** Pattaya has invested heavily in CCTV "Smart Safety Zones" around Walking
Street, Jomtien and the malls, and Tourist Police presence is high. Standard rules still apply —
don't leave a drink unattended, use hotel lockers, don't walk unlit sois at 3 a.m., and let
someone at home know your route. Tourist Police: **1155**. Emergency: **191**. Ambulance: **1669**.

## Practical setup

- **eSIM:** buy AIS, TrueMove or dtac at the airport or as an eSIM before you fly. ~฿300–600
  for 15–30 days with generous data. Coverage is excellent everywhere on this itinerary.
- **Apps:** Grab (taxis + food delivery, the single most useful app), Google Maps, Bolt,
  Agoda (better Thai inventory than Booking), 12Go (buses/trains/ferries), Google Translate
  with Thai downloaded offline, XE.
- **Power:** Type A/B/C sockets, 220 V — your Israeli type-H plug **will not fit**. Bring a
  universal adapter.
- **Packing for monsoon:** a proper rain jacket beats an umbrella in wind; quick-dry clothing;
  **two pairs of shoes** so one can always be drying; flip-flops; a dry bag or waterproof phone
  pouch; long trousers and a shoulder-covering top for temples (enforced, not suggested);
  DEET; a light fleece for Pai and Doi Inthanon mornings, which genuinely get cold.
- **Laundry:** ฿40–60/kg everywhere, same day. Pack for one week, not four.

## Etiquette, briefly

Feet are dirty and heads are sacred: never point your feet at a person or a Buddha image, never
touch anyone's head. Take shoes off entering homes and temples. Return a *wai* (palms together)
from someone older or in service — but you don't initiate one to staff or children. **Never lose
your temper in public**; raising your voice makes you lose the argument in Thai eyes, instantly
and permanently. Learn *sawasdee khrap* (hello) and *khop khun khrap* (thank you) — the "khrap"
is the male ending and it will genuinely change how you're treated.

## Book this week (you are ~3.5 weeks out)

1. **International flight TLV↔BKK** — El Al flies this daily nonstop (~11 h), Arkia twice
   weekly. Prices climb sharply inside 21 days. **Book first, today.**
2. **Travel clinic appointment** — vaccine lead times.
3. **Travel insurance** — with the motorbike clause checked.
4. **Domestic flights** (UTP or BKK → CNX on 1 Sep; CNX → BKK on 18 Sep) — cheap now,
   3× the price at the airport.
5. **Elephant sanctuary** — the one activity that sells out.
6. **First 3 nights in Bangkok** — so you have an address for the TDAC form.
7. Everything else can wait. Low season means walk-up availability is genuine, and flexibility
   is worth more than a locked-in booking when you're chasing gaps in the rain.
8. **18–19 August:** submit the TDAC (72-hour window opens).
